# Statistics on the `x * y` split

The conjecture said the remainder breaks into `x * y` with `x` a prime factor. `x` is *always* a proper divisor; here is how often it is also prime.

| property of a non-zero residue | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| `x = n/gcd(n,k)` is a proper divisor of `n` | 27,556 | 100.000% |
| `x` divides the residue | 27,556 | 100.000% |
| `gcd(residue, n)` is a proper divisor | 27,556 | 100.000% |
| `x` is prime | 9,920 | 35.999% |
| `y = 1` (residue is exactly `x`) | 11,841 | 42.971% |

At the **first** non-zero position specifically, `x = n/spf(n)` is prime for **186/489 = 38.0%** of composites below 600 -- exactly the semiprimes and prime squares, since `n/spf(n)` is prime iff `n` is a product of two primes.

So the conjecture is right in the form that matters: for a semiprime `n = p*q` the first non-zero remainder is **exactly `q`**, a prime factor, with `y = 1`.
