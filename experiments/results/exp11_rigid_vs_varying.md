# Rigid versus varying group orders, counted

How many distinct group orders can each construction reach at one fixed `p`?

### The setup

Every method here succeeds on a prime `p` exactly when some integer attached to `p` is smooth. The question is how many *different* such integers a construction can offer at one fixed `p`.

- **AKS ring**: `(F_p[x]/f)*` has order `prod_i (p^deg(f_i) - 1)` over the irreducible factors of `f`. Varying `f` over monic degree `d` can only permute the multiset of factor degrees, so the count of distinct orders is capped by `partitions(d)` -- a constant, independent of `p`.
- **Elliptic curves**: `#E(F_p) = p + 1 - t` with `|t| <= 2*sqrt(p)`, so the orders range over an interval of width `4*sqrt(p)`.

### Measured

| p | ring d=2 (found/cap) | ring d=3 | ring d=4 | distinct #E(F_p) | Hasse width 4*sqrt(p) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 211 | 2 / 2 | 3 / 3 | 5 / 5 | 55 | 56 |
| 503 | 2 / 2 | 3 / 3 | 5 / 5 | 84 | 88 |
| 1009 | 2 / 2 | 3 / 3 | 5 / 5 | 109 | 124 |
| 2003 | 2 / 2 | 3 / 3 | 5 / 5 | 140 | 176 |

The ring columns saturate at `partitions(d)` and **stop**: 2 orders at `d = 2`, 3 at `d = 3`, 5 at `d = 4`, no matter how large `p` grows or how many `f` are tried. The elliptic column grows with `p`, tracking the Hasse interval.

### Why the cap is structural, not an artifact of sampling

`F_p[x]/f` for squarefree `f` is a product of finite fields `F_{p^{d_1}} x ... x F_{p^{d_m}}` with `sum d_i = d`. Its unit group order is therefore `prod_i (p^{d_i} - 1)`, a function of the *partition* `(d_1, ..., d_m)` alone. Two polynomials with the same degree pattern give literally the same order. There is nothing left to vary.

The same holds for any subgroup singled out by a norm condition: Theorem 13's norm-one subgroup has order `(p^d - 1)/(p - 1)`, again a function of `p` and `d` only. Restricting to a subgroup changes *which* rigid number you get, never the fact that it is rigid.

### What it costs

If `Phi_e(p)` is rough for every `e <= d`, then every construction in this repository fails at that `p`, and no additional work at that `p` helps -- you have exhausted the finitely many orders available. The only recourse is to raise `d`, and the cost of working in degree `d` grows while the supply of new orders grows only like `partitions(d)`.

ECM never faces that wall: a fresh curve is a fresh order, drawn from an interval of `~4*sqrt(p)` candidates, so one keeps sampling until a smooth order appears. Balancing sampling cost against smoothness density is exactly what produces `L[1/2]`.

### The gap, stated as a requirement

A polynomial-time method in this family would need a construction over `Z/n` whose group order at a fixed prime `p` varies with a parameter under our control. Products of the multiplicative groups of finite fields -- which is all that quotients of `(Z/n)[x]` supply -- cannot do it: their orders are pinned to `prod (p^{d_i} - 1)` by the degree partition. Escaping requires a genuinely different algebraic group, which is what an elliptic curve is and what no rearrangement of Pascal's triangle will produce.
