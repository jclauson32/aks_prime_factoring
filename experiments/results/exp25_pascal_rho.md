# Pascal rho: the triangle on the collision mechanism

Round 17. The fourth mechanism, and what Pascal's reflection does to it.

## 1. `C(x, 2)` is Pollard's rho

With `x = 2y + 1/2`, `C(x, 2) = 2(y^2 - 5/16) + 1/2`: iterating the second column of the triangle is iterating `y -> y^2 - 5/16`, Pollard's map with `c = -5/16`. Checked on `F_p` for `p = 101` (ok), `p = 1009` (ok), `p = 65537` (ok), `p = 1000003` (ok).

## 2. Pascal's reflection sets the collision rate

For a map `f` on `F_p` the expected rho length is `sqrt(pi p / (2 kappa))`, where `kappa = (1/p) sum_v m_v(m_v - 1)` over the fibre sizes `m_v = #f^(-1)(v)`. A random map has `kappa = 1`. The rows of the triangle are palindromes, which as polynomials reads `C(k-1-x, k) = (-1)^k C(x, k)`: for even `k` the map factors through `(x - (k-1)/2)^2`, each fibre is closed under the reflection, and `kappa` doubles.

| 2 | 1.000 | 1 | 1.253 +- 0.035 | 1.253 | +0.0 |
| 3 | 1.000 | 1 | 1.279 +- 0.039 | 1.253 | +0.7 |
| 4 | 2.000 | 2 | 0.900 +- 0.024 | 0.886 | +0.6 |

Every row agrees with the prediction (largest deviation 0.7 standard errors). Even columns from `k = 4` on collide `sqrt 2` times sooner.

That is the effect Brent and Pollard exploited with `x^(2^j) + c` for Fermat numbers, arising here from the symmetry of the triangle and for every `p`, not only `p = 1 (mod 2^j)`. Section 3 prices it.

## 3. Factoring with it

Brent's cycle finder on `x -> C(x, k) mod N`, gcds batched 64 at a time. `evaluations` counts every application of the map, Brent's advance loop included.

| bits of p and q | semiprimes | evaluations / sqrt(min p), k=2 | k=4 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 30 | 1.74 | 1.18 | 0.68 |
| 24 | 30 | 1.69 | 1.13 | 0.67 |
| 28 | 30 | 1.39 | 1.26 | 0.90 |

Pascal rho factors at `N^(1/4)` like Pollard's rho, because it is Pollard's rho. The even column needs 0.75 times the evaluations of `k = 2` (the fibre theory says `1/sqrt 2 = 0.71`; these are 30-sample means of a heavy-tailed quantity).

In multiplications: `C(x, 2)` conjugates to `y^2 - 5/16`, one squaring per step; `C(x, 4)` conjugates to `((u^2 - 5/4)^2 - 37)/24`, two squarings and a constant. With the gcd accumulator that is 2 against 3 or 4 multiplications per step, so per unit of `sqrt p` the even column costs `3/sqrt 2 = 2.12` at best against `2`: **the reflection's `sqrt 2` is paid back in full, and then some.**

## 4. The map: Pascal's triangle on all four mechanisms

| mechanism | Pascal object | cost here | best known for the mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| order | the row `(1+x)^N` mod `(N, f)`; the AKS fold (rounds 1-5, 9-10) | rigid orders, one ticket per `p` | `L[1/2]` (ECM, varying curves) |
| size | first non-zero entry, factorial threshold (T4, T20) | `O~(N^(1/4))` | `N^(1/5)` (Harvey) |
| sign | central column `sum C(2k,k) x^k` (round 15) | `O~(N^(1/4))`, ~15x Strassen | `L[1/3]` (NFS) |
| collision | column map `x -> C(x, k)` (this round) | `O(N^(1/4))`, = Pollard rho | `N^(1/4)` (rho) |

The triangle hosts every mechanism, and on each one it lands at that mechanism's *baseline* -- never at its best. The accelerations are all outside it: ECM's speed comes from redrawing the group, Harvey's from Lehman's geometry, NFS's from smoothness in a number field. Pascal's triangle is a universal host for factoring mechanisms and an accelerator of none of them.
