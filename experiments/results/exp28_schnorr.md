# Schnorr's prime-number lattice, tested

Round 20. Does lattice reduction find smooth relations better than chance?

## Setup

For primes `p_1 .. p_n`, the lattice spanned by `b_i = (0, .., f_i, .., 0, round(C ln p_i))` with `f` a random permutation of `ceil(i/2)` and `C = N`, target `t = (0, .., 0, round(C ln N))`. Kannan's embedding and an exact integer LLL give close vectors `sum e_i b_i`; each yields `u = prod p_i^(e_i > 0)`, `v = prod p_i^(-e_i < 0)` and `r = u - vN`. A smooth `r` is a relation `u = r (mod N)`; `n + 2` of them factor `N` by linear algebra mod 2.

## A structural handicap

`u` and `v` use disjoint primes, and `r` is divisible by none of them: `p | u` forces `r = -vN != 0 (mod p)`, and `p | v` forces `r = u`. Checked on 1249 of 1249 candidates. A close vector that uses half the factor base leaves `r` only the other half to be smooth over.

There is a partial compensation at the primes the vector did *not* use. There `u` and `v` are units, so `p^k | r` with probability `1/phi(p^k)` rather than `1/p^k` -- at `p = 2`, `r` is always even. The fair baseline therefore draws integers within 10% of `|r|` from exactly this local law at every prime below 60, coprime to every prime the vector used.

Across the table: **62** smooth residues against **68.5** expected from the fair baseline (Poisson z = -0.8) and 191.1 from the naive one. The lattice-found residues are as smooth as integers of their size with the same local law, and no smoother -- the lattice buys a smaller `r`, and nothing about its factorisation.

The baseline took three attempts, and the first two were wrong in instructive ways. Integers of the same size predict 3.1 times too many smooth residues -- they ignore the handicap. Adding only coprimality and parity predicts 55.2 (z = +0.9): it omits the unit effect at 3, 5, 7, ..., which biases it 19% low -- enough, in a larger sample, to pass for a lattice advantage. With the full local law the difference is z = -0.8.

And the size it buys is not small: the median residue is larger than `N` in 5 of 5 rows. The relation yield falls from 0.199 per lattice at 32 bits to 0.013 at 40 bits and 0.000 at 44, while the number of relations needed grows with the factor base. The method works -- it factored the 32-bit number -- at a cost set by the smoothness probability of the residues it finds; Dixon's random squares, by comparison, have residues below `N`.

This is a test at toy sizes, with LLL and Kannan's embedding rather than the BKZ and pruned enumeration Schnorr proposed; stronger reduction finds closer vectors and smaller residues. What it cannot change is the structural handicap, which is a property of the residues and not of the solver, nor the absence of a smoothness bias beyond it.
