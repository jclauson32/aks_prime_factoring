# Shor's algorithm, simulated classically

Round 24. The order mechanism without the smoothness condition, and what it costs to fake.

## 1. The interference pattern

`N = 21`, `a = 2`, order `r = 6`, first register `t = 9` qubits (`Q = 512`). After measuring `a^x mod N = 8`, the Fourier transform puts the first register's probability on multiples of `Q/r = 85.3`:

| outcome y | probability | y r / Q | order read by continued fractions |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.166 | 0.000 | None |
| 85 | 0.114 | 0.996 | 6 |
| 171 | 0.114 | 2.004 | 6 |
| 256 | 0.166 | 3.000 | 6 |
| 341 | 0.114 | 3.996 | 6 |
| 427 | 0.114 | 5.004 | 6 |

The 6 peaks hold 79% of the probability. Outcomes `y` with `gcd(y r / Q, r) = 1` give `r` directly; the others give a divisor, recovered by trying small multiples.

At these sizes the orders are small, hence smooth, and Pollard's `p - 1` would have found them too; the simulation cannot reach the sizes where the difference shows. What it does show is that nothing in the procedure asks whether `r` is smooth: the Fourier transform reads `r` whatever its factorisation.

## 3. Where the cost went

The simulation's time per run follows `Q = 2^t ~ N^2`: from `N = 667` to `N = 1517` the register grew 8x, from `2^19` to `2^22` amplitudes, and the time per run 9.3x. That is `N^2` -- worse than trial division's `N^(1/2)` -- because a classical machine has to write down every amplitude.

| resource | quantum computer | this simulation |
|---|---|---|
| qubits / memory | `t ~ 2 log2 N` qubits | `2^t ~ N^2` complex amplitudes |
| modular exponentiation | `O(t)` multiplications, applied to all `x` at once | `2^t` evaluations of `a^x mod N` |
| Fourier transform | `O(t^2)` gates | an FFT of length `2^t` |
| total | polynomial in `log N` | `Theta(N^2 log N)` |

That is the exact shape of the gap. The order mechanism needs `r`; classically `r` can only be used blind, as an exponent divisible by it, which is why smoothness decides everything. Interference reads `r` off a superposition of all `Q` exponents. Nothing in twenty-three classical rounds found a way to read it without writing the `Q` values down, and by Miller's reduction, reading it any other way would factor `N`.
