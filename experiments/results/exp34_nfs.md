# A toy number field sieve

Round 26. The sign mechanism with the smallest numbers anyone knows how to make smooth.

## 1. It works

| bits of N | NFS factored | NFS time (median) | QS time (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 3 of 3 | 0.2 s | 0.01 s |
| 50 | 3 of 3 | 0.3 s | 0.01 s |
| 60 | 3 of 3 | 1.0 s | 0.03 s |
| 70 | 3 of 3 | 4.8 s | 0.10 s |
| 80 | 2 of 2 | 18.3 s | 0.57 s |

Every dependency that survives the rational check goes through the number-field square root, `beta^2 = f'(alpha)^2 prod (a - b alpha)` in `Z[alpha]`, lifted from an inert prime; `gcd(phi(beta) - f'(m) X, N)` then splits `N`. At these sizes the quadratic sieve is faster, as it is in practice until about a hundred decimal digits.

## 2. Why it wins eventually: the sizes that must be smooth

| bits of N | QS: bits of |Q(x)|, nominal sieve length 2 x 10^4 | NFS: bits of |a - bm| |N(a - b alpha)|, median of relations found | NFS minus QS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 45 | 54 | +9 |
| 80 | 55 | 65 | +10 |
| 100 | 65 | 80 | +15 |

At degree 3 and these sizes the two numbers the NFS needs smooth are, together, 9 to 15 bits *larger* than the one the quadratic sieve needs -- so it finds relations more slowly and loses, as the timings show. Its advantage is asymptotic: with the degree raised as `d ~ (3 log N / log log N)^(1/3)`, the product shrinks to `L[2/3]` in size against the sieve's `N^(1/2 + o(1))`, and the smoothness probability of `L[2/3]`-sized numbers gives the `L[1/3]` running time. In practice the crossover is near a hundred decimal digits.

That completes the list. Each of round 14's four mechanisms now has its best method implemented here and run on the repository's own numbers: ECM for order, Harvey for size, rho for collision, and the quadratic and number field sieves for sign. The fastest of them, the number field sieve, is sub-exponential and not polynomial, because smooth numbers of size `L[2/3]` are only `L[1/3]`-rare.
