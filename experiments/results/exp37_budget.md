Best at each budget: 100 -- Pollard p - 1 (order, rigid); 1,000 -- Pollard p - 1 (order, rigid); 10,000 -- Pollard p - 1 (order, rigid); 100,000 -- ECM, best B1 for the budget (order, redrawn).

The order of overtaking is set by how each mechanism's probability grows with effort. `p - 1` pays immediately -- its first few small primes are the ones most likely to divide `p - 1` -- and then saturates at the fraction of primes whose `p - 1` is smooth enough to reach. Rho pays nothing until its walk approaches `sqrt p` steps (about 12,288 multiplications here) and then pays everything. ECM spends its budget on many cheap curves and climbs steadily, because every curve is a fresh draw. Trial division is linear in the budget and needs `p` of them.

This is the curve selection climbed in round 28: at a budget of a few dozen multiplications, the only mechanism with a gradient is the order mechanism with a fixed group, so that is what evolution found -- and the reason it stopped there is visible in the same table, since the mechanisms that overtake it do so only after thousands of multiplications.
