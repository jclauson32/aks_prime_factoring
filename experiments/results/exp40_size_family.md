# The size mechanism, by coverage per operation

Round 34. How many candidate factors each method rules out per unit of work.

## Coverage per second

Each method is given a range of candidates to rule out and timed on a number with no factor in that range, so nothing stops early. The measure is candidates ruled out per second.

| bits of N | trial division (candidates/s) | product tree, factorial mod n (candidates/s) | hyperbola hull walk (rows/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 48 | 2,525,089 | 19,891,703 | 115,799,423 |
| 60 | 2,588,952 | 67,776,614 | 549,879,070 |

The product tree wins by batching: one modular multiplication absorbs a whole block of candidates, so its rate per second is far above trial division's even though both are `Theta(p)` in candidates. The hull walk covers rows of the hyperbola rather than candidate divisors -- each vertex settles a stretch of about `N^(1/6)` rows -- which is why it reaches `N^(1/3)` while the others are `N^(1/2)`.

## What each change buys

| method | what one operation covers | cost |
|---|---|---|
| trial division, Pascal row scan | one candidate | `Theta(p)` |
| Pollard-Strassen product tree (T20) | a batch, by multipoint evaluation | `O~(N^(1/4))` |
| Lehman / the hull walk (P23) | an interval of the hyperbola near a rational slope | `O~(N^(1/3))` |
| Harvey's method | Lehman's intervals plus a Fermat congruence and a BSGS sweep | `N^(1/5)` |
| Coppersmith with a hint | everything, once `N^(1/4)` of `p` is known | polynomial, given the bits |

The last row is the one that matters for a breakthrough, and round 12 priced it: Coppersmith needs about a quarter of `p`'s bits (65 of them for a 256-bit `N`), and guessing them costs `N^(1/4)` -- exactly what the other size methods already cost. The exponent `beta^2` in Coppersmith's bound is what would have to move.

| beta (p ~ N^beta) | Coppersmith window | guess + Coppersmith | Strassen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | N^0.250 | N^0.250 | N^0.250 |
| 0.40 | N^0.160 | N^0.240 | N^0.200 |
| 0.30 | N^0.090 | N^0.210 | N^0.150 |

Every size method in this repository is a different way of paying the same `N^(1/4)`-to-`N^(1/3)` bill for locating `p` within a window Coppersmith can finish from. None of them is cheaper than the batching bound, and the one that is faster -- Harvey's `N^(1/5)` -- gets there by combining the geometry with a congruence, not by covering more candidates per operation.
