# Bits two moduli share, and what they are worth

The prediction is the Gaussian heuristic and nothing more: the wanted vector has norm about `2^alpha sqrt k`, a generic shortest vector of this lattice has norm `det^(1/k) = 2^(t (k-1)/k)`, and the attack works exactly when the first is the smaller. More moduli lower the bar towards `alpha` and never past it.

## Two controls

The first asks whether the lattice is doing anything at all: the same code on moduli whose primes share nothing. The second is the case that matters cryptographically -- balanced `p` and `q`, where the cofactor is as long as `p`, so the threshold exceeds the length of `p` itself and no amount of sharing can reach it.

| control | what the heuristic says | factored (of 20) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 unrelated moduli, `t = 100` claimed | no short vector: the wanted one is not in the lattice | 0 |
| 8 balanced moduli (60-bit `p` and `q`), sharing 48 of 60 bits | threshold 69 > 60 bits of `p` | 0 |

The second row is the reason this is not an attack on RSA. 48 of 60 bits of `p` shared between eight moduli is already an absurd amount of correlation, and it is still short of the threshold, because with `q` as long as `p` that threshold (69 bits) is longer than `p` itself (60 bits) -- at every `k`, since `alpha k/(k-1)` is strictly greater than `alpha`.

## What it costs

| k | lattice | entry size (bits) | one attack |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 x 2 | 100 | 0.1 ms |
| 3 | 3 x 3 | 100 | 0.2 ms |
| 5 | 5 x 5 | 100 | 1.0 ms |
| 8 | 8 x 8 | 100 | 7.0 ms |

Polynomial in everything, because the lattice is small and its entries are `t` bits, not `log N` bits. When the condition holds the answer is immediate; when it fails, no budget helps. That is the same shape as Coppersmith in round 12 -- a lattice that either has the vector or does not.

## What a shared bit is worth

| what you are given | how much of it is needed | who finishes |
|---|---|---|
| nothing | -- | `N^(1/4)` work (rounds 33-36) |
| known bits of `p`, value and all | a quarter of `log N` (round 12) | Coppersmith |
| bits of `p` shared with one other modulus, value unknown | more than `2 alpha` -- 80 here | this lattice |
| the same, shared with `k` moduli | more than `alpha k/(k-1)`, tending to `alpha` | this lattice |

A shared bit is worth less than a known bit, and the exchange rate is the interesting number. Coppersmith finishes from a quarter of `log N`; this lattice needs the shared part to be longer than the whole cofactor it is solving for -- for a balanced modulus, more than half of `log N`, which is more than the secret itself. Correlation between instances is real information and it is measurably cheaper to exploit than to acquire, but it is priced above the same wall: what has to be supplied is always at least the size of what is being hidden.
