# How far the wall is, in years

Round 41. Fitting this repository's sieves and extrapolating them, calibrated against the published records.

## What this repository's sieves cost

Balanced semiprimes, median of three, on one core of whatever machine this ran on. The quadratic sieve is the one that reaches furthest here; the number field sieve is a toy at degree 3 and loses to it at every size it can manage, which is a fact about the implementation and not about the algorithms.

## Fitting the shape

The model is `t = A exp(c (ln N)^alpha (ln ln N)^(1-alpha))` with `alpha` held at the theoretical value, fitted as a straight line in the log. Both parameters are needed: forced through the origin, `c` silently absorbs the implementation's constant factor `A`, which is the one quantity this round has to keep separate.

| method | alpha | fitted c | fitted A (seconds) | textbook c |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| quadratic sieve | 1/2 | 0.688 | 1.57e-05 | 1 |
| number field sieve (toy, degree 3) | 1/3 | 0.917 | 5.50e-04 | 1.923 |

| bits of N | measured | fitted | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 0.12 s | 0.08 s | 0.67 |
| 70 | 0.10 s | 0.20 s | 1.89 |
| 80 | 0.45 s | 0.45 s | 0.99 |
| 90 | 1.24 s | 0.98 s | 0.79 |
| 100 | 2.03 s | 2.06 s | 1.02 |

The fit's worst residual inside the measured range is a factor of 1.89. A curve that is already off by that much where it was fitted cannot be read to two decimal places outside it, so what follows uses it only for orders of magnitude -- and then stops using it altogether.

## Extrapolating this implementation

| bits of N | this repository's QS (years, one core) | this repository's toy NFS (years, one core) |
|---|---|---|
| 128 | 4.51e-07 | 1.18e-06 |
| 256 | 5.60e-04 | 8.71e-05 |
| 512 | 2.13e+01 | 2.62e-02 |
| 1024 | 1.20e+08 | 4.97e+01 |

Those numbers are worthless and it is worth saying why, because the reason is the whole difficulty with extrapolation. The toy sieve's fitted `c` is 0.917 against a textbook 1.923, so the curve bends far too gently and 'predicts' RSA-768 in 1.7 core-years. A constant fitted over 30 bits of range does not survive 700 bits of extrapolation. The next section discards these fits entirely.

## Calibrating on the records instead

The published factorisations are the honest data for an extrapolation to real key sizes: a serious number field sieve, on hardware that existed, with the effort reported. Fitting `A` to each record with `c` held at the textbook 1.923 gives a constant per record, and the useful check is whether the two records agree on it -- they are 61 bits and ten years apart, so if the `L[1/3]` shape were wrong between them, they would not.

| record | bits | published core-years | implied A (seconds) at c = 1.923 |
|---|---|---|---|
| RSA-768 (2009) | 768 | 2,000 | 5.86e-13 |
| RSA-250 (2020) | 829 | 2,700 | 1.30e-13 |

The two implied constants differ by a factor of 4.52. Ten years of hardware and software progress, and 61 bits, and the `L[1/3]` shape absorbs the difference to within that -- which is the evidence that extrapolating along this curve is a reasonable thing to do at all.

| bits of N | core-years at the records' quality | years on a million cores | years on a million cores, after a million-fold speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1024 | 1.15e+06 | 1.15e+00 | 1.15e-06 |
| 2048 | 1.34e+15 | 1.34e+09 | 1.34e+03 |
| 4096 | 1.13e+27 | 1.13e+21 | 1.13e+15 |

So one doubling of the key, from 1024 to 2048 bits, costs nine orders of magnitude. That is the shape of `L[1/3]`, and it is the whole security argument for RSA -- not that factoring is hard, but that it gets hard faster than keys get long.

It also says where constants still matter. At 1024 bits the total is within reach of a large enough machine for long enough, which is why 1024-bit RSA is deprecated rather than merely discouraged; a million-fold improvement in constants would finish it outright. The same million-fold leaves 2048 bits at a billion core-years. Near the edge, constants decide; past it, only the exponent does.

Which is why the search in this project was always for a change of exponent. Rounds 33 to 36 found four mechanisms and one exponent each. Rounds 37 to 40 found three kinds of extra information -- bits shared between moduli, batches of instances, a predicate that splits the range -- and priced every one of them above the wall. The table above is the size of the prize for moving it.
