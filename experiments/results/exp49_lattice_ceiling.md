# How much window more lattice buys

Round 43. The reachable Coppersmith window as a function of the lattice's dimension: rising, with diminishing returns, towards 1/4.

## What one more dimension buys

5 balanced semiprimes of 64 bits, with `m` stopped at 10: the lattice is `2m`-dimensional in exact arithmetic and the next step up costs more than this round is worth. For each lattice parameter `m`, the largest window `2^t` from which the lattice still recovers `p`, found by bisection (a smaller window is strictly easier, so the property is monotone and bisection is honest). The window is reported as an exponent of `N`, which is the quantity Coppersmith's theorem bounds by `beta^2 = 1/4`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 0 | 0.000 | 0.000-0.000 | 0.00 s |
| 2 | 4 | 11 | 0.172 | 0.156-0.219 | 0.01 s |
| 3 | 6 | 14 | 0.219 | 0.203-0.234 | 0.03 s |
| 4 | 8 | 14 | 0.219 | 0.219-0.250 | 0.08 s |
| 5 | 10 | 15 | 0.234 | 0.219-0.234 | 0.18 s |
| 6 | 12 | 15 | 0.234 | 0.219-0.234 | 0.58 s |
| 8 | 16 | 15 | 0.234 | 0.234-0.266 | 4.98 s |
| 10 | 20 | 15 | 0.234 | 0.234-0.266 | 29.91 s |

The reachable exponent climbs with `m` and flattens: `m = 1` reaches `N^0.000`, `m = 2` reaches `N^0.172`, `m = 3` reaches `N^0.219`, `m = 4` reaches `N^0.219`, `m = 5` reaches `N^0.234`, `m = 6` reaches `N^0.234`, `m = 8` reaches `N^0.234`, `m = 10` reaches `N^0.234`. The ceiling the theorem gives is `N^0.250`, and the best measured here is `N^0.234`.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | +0.172 | 2 |

Each doubling of the lattice buys less than the last, which is what `eps(m) ~ 1/m` looks like from underneath. The medians stay under `1/4`, but individual instances reach `N^0.266`, a shade over it. That is what an asymptotic bound with an `-eps` looks like at 64 bits: the window is quantised to whole bits, one bit is 0.016 of the exponent here, and a lucky instance clears the bar by one. It is the theorem's error term, not a counterexample to it.

## What the ceiling is worth

Suppose the exponent were `1/4 + delta` instead. Round 38's algorithm walks a tree of `2^t` leaves to feed this lattice, so its cost is exactly the window's reciprocal: `N^(1/4)` leaves today, `N^(1/4 - delta)` then. The table below is that arithmetic, and it is the reason this one exponent keeps reappearing.

| Coppersmith exponent | cost of the tree that feeds it | what it would beat |
|---|---|---|
| 0.250 | N^0.250 | where every size method already is |
| 0.260 | N^0.240 | beats the `N^(1/4)` batching methods |
| 0.300 | N^0.200 | ties Harvey's deterministic `N^(1/5)` |
| 0.333 | N^0.167 | beats the best deterministic exponent known |

At `delta = 1/12` the window is `N^(1/3)`, the tree is `N^(1/6)`, and the deterministic record falls. The whole size family, in this project and in the literature, is the search for that `delta`, and forty-three rounds have not moved it.
