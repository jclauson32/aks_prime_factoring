# Four ways a modulus can be weak, each priced

Round 44. Close primes, smooth `p - 1`, structured primes, a shared prime in a corpus -- all generator failures, all cheap, none of them factoring.

## 1. The primes are too close: Fermat

`N = ((p+q)/2)^2 - ((q-p)/2)^2`, so a search upward from `sqrt N` for a perfect square finds the factorisation after about `(q-p)^2 / (8 sqrt N)` steps. The gap is the whole cost.

| gap asked for | gap obtained | Fermat steps | predicted steps | factored |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2^8 | 264 | 1 | 0 | yes |
| 2^12 | 4,106 | 1 | 0 | yes |
| 2^16 | 65,546 | 1 | 0 | yes |
| 2^20 | 1,048,578 | 52 | 51 | yes |
| 2^24 | 16,777,248 | 10,209 | 10,209 | yes |

The control: two independent 32-bit primes, gap about `2^31`, and Fermat is still searching after 200,000 steps. Predicted for that gap is about 740,927. Nothing about `N` has changed -- only how its primes were chosen.

## 2. `p - 1` is smooth: Pollard

| 100 | yes | 0.0 ms |

Round 35 priced the draw itself: about 40% of 15-bit primes have a 200-powersmooth `p - 1`, and the share falls with the size of `p`, which is why this is a generator's job and not an attack.

## 3. The primes carry structure: ROCA in miniature

Infineon's RSALib generated primes as `p = k M + 65537^a mod M` with `M` a primorial. That makes `p mod M` known up to the `a`, and `a` ranges only over the order of 65537 mod `M` -- so if `M >= N^(1/4)`, round 12's lattice finishes from each guess. CVE-2017-15361; the version here is the same shape with a small `M`.

| modulus | bits | `M` | lattice `m` | guesses for `a` | lattice calls | factored | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `p = kM + 65537^a mod M` | 64 | 510,510 | 5 | 24 | 14 | yes | 0.74 s |
| control: both primes random | 64 | 510,510 | 5 | 24 | 24 | no | 1.32 s |

The window handed to the lattice is `2^13.9` -- twice `sqrt(N)/M`, because the structured prime may be either side of `sqrt N` and the attacker knows the key size but not which. Round 43 measured what that costs in lattice: `m = 3` reaches only `N^0.203` and fails on most instances here, `m = 5` reaches `N^0.234` and does not. That is a prediction from one round deciding a parameter in another, and it is why the table above says `m = 5`.

`M = 510,510` is `2^19.0`, above `N^(1/4)` for a 64-bit modulus, and 65537 has order 24 there -- so the entire keyspace of this generator is 24 lattice calls wide. The real flaw used a much larger primorial and needed Coppersmith's trick of substituting a divisor of `M`, but the accounting is this one: a generator that leaks `p mod M` for `M >= N^(1/4)` has given away exactly what round 12 priced at `N^(1/4)` of work.

## 4. Two moduli share a prime: batch gcd

| method | gcds | time |
|---|---|---|
| batch gcd (one product tree) | 2,000 | 0.05 s |
| every pair | 1,999,000 | 0.42 s (projected from 200) |

2 of the 2,000 moduli came back with a nontrivial gcd, sharing 1 distinct prime, and the planted prime is among them -- both victims are factored by their own gcd, without anyone knowing in advance which two moduli to compare. This is Heninger, Durumeric, Wustrow and Halderman's survey of the live web, which found tens of thousands of hosts whose keys fell to exactly this, for exactly this reason: entropy failures at boot.

## What the four have in common

| failure | what leaked | cost of the attack | cost without it |
|---|---|---|---|
| primes too close | `(q-p)` is small | `(q-p)^2 / sqrt N` steps | `N^(1/4)` |
| `p - 1` smooth | the order of `(Z/p)*` factors small | one exponentiation | `L[1/2]` with redraws (round 35) |
| structured primes (ROCA) | `p mod M` for a known `M >= N^(1/4)` | `ord_M(65537)` lattice calls | `N^(1/4)` (round 12) |
| a shared prime | one prime, twice, in a public corpus | one gcd, or `O~(k)` for the whole corpus | `L[1/3]` per modulus |
| shared low bits (round 37) | `t > alpha k/(k-1)` bits of `p` | one LLL | `L[1/3]` per modulus |

Every row is a property of the generator, and every attack reads something the generator was supposed to keep random. None of them is an attack on factoring: give any of these methods a modulus whose primes were drawn independently and uniformly, and the controls above are what happens -- nothing.

Which is the same sentence this project has arrived at from every other direction. The hardness is not in the multiplication. It is in having nothing to say about `p` except that `p` divides `N`.
